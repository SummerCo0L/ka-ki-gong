# Student Care Fee Assistance (SCFA)

*Source: https://supportgowhere.life.gov.sg/schemes/SCFA*

Provides fee assistance for children from lower-income working families enrolled in Student Care Centres (SCCs) registered with MSF.

## Highlights
- Subsidies of up to $290 per month for children enrolled in SCCs , and co-payments from $11 per month for children enrolled in Special SCCs (SSCCs)
- One-time Start-Up Grant (SUG) of up to $400 per child
- Child must attend an SCC registered with MSF

**Who it targets:** Families, Low-income
**Keywords:** Budget 2026, Students, Subsidies

## About this support
- For children attending SCCs aged 7 to 14 years old (Primary 1 to Secondary 2) in the calendar year in which subsidies commence, the subsidy is up to $290 per month, depending on your monthly household income.
- For children attending SSCCs aged 7 to 18 years old in the calendar year in which subsidies commence, the out-of-pocket amount from $11 per month, depending on your monthly household income. Please visit the [Enabling Guide by SG Enable](https://www.enablingguide.sg/im-looking-for-disability-support/child-adult-care/special-student-care-centres) for more information on the subsidies.

## Who is this for?
- Child is a Singapore Citizen or a Permanent Resident (at least one immediate family member in the same household must be a Singapore Citizen)
- Both you and your spouse are each working at least 56 hours a month
- Child must attend a Student Care Centre registered with MSF
- For children attending SCCs aged 7 to 14 years old (Primary 1 to Secondary 2) in the calendar year in which subsidies commence, the monthly gross household income must be $4,500 or less for families with 4 or fewer members, or monthly household income per capita must be $1,125 or less for families with 5 or more members. From January 2027: The monthly gross household income must be $6,500 or less, or monthly household income per capita must be $1,625 or less.
  For children attending SSCCs aged 7 to 18 years old in the calendar year in which subsidies commence, the monthly gross household income must be $9,200 or less for families with 4 or fewer members, or monthly household income per capita must be $2,300 or less for families with 5 more members. Please visit [Enabling Guide by SG Enable](https://www.enablingguide.sg/im-looking-for-disability-support/child-adult-care/special-student-care-centres) for more information on the subsidies.

**How Income is Calculated**

- For salaried employees:
Based on average gross monthly income over 12 months from CPF records or the latest IRAS assessment (within the last 2 years).
Includes: basic salary, bonuses, commissions, overtime pay, allowances, cash rewards, and employee CPF contributions.
- For self-employed individuals:
Based on average trade income over 12 months from the latest IRAS assessment (within the last 2 years), or income declared to CPF Board.

**Note:** Family members refer to the child’s grandparents or siblings below 21 years old who live at the same address, are not working or have no income, and are Singapore Citizens or Permanent Residents.

Your gross monthly income will be determined via HOMES as part of the means-test.

For further information, please refer to [MSF | Student Care Fee Assistance](https://www.msf.gov.sg/what-we-do/student-care/for-parents/student-care-fee-assistance).

## How to apply?
All SCFA applications must be submitted online via [SupportGoWhere](https://supportgowhere.life.gov.sg/grants/scfa)  at least six months before the intended start date. For example, if you wish to apply for SCFA effective from January 2027, you may submit the application from July 2026 onwards. SCFA approval will commence from the month in which the application is received, or from the month following the expiry of any existing SCFA, whichever is later.

[Click here to learn more about the SCFA application process.](https://www.msf.gov.sg/docs/default-source/student-care/scfa-infographic---application-process.pdf)

- If you're unemployed or looking for work, you will need to provide supporting documents (for example, proof of job search, medical letter, or medical certificate).
- With the SCFA, if you still face difficulties with the monthly co-payment of your child's student care fees, you may submit an appeal through the SCC, along with supporting documents detailing the change in circumstances and an explanation of the reasons for the appeal. Appeals will be considered on a case-by-case basis, and if approved, will commence from the month when the appeal was submitted
